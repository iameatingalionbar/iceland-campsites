Ring Road Campsites in Order (2026): Iceland's Route 1 Clockwise from Reykjavík, with Map and Winter Status

Route 1, the Ring Road, has been 1,309 km long since 24 June 2026, when a new section across Hornafjörður opened and shortened it by 12 km (from 1,321 km). It is paved all the way and passes through almost every part of Iceland except the Westfjords.[1][2]

This guide lists the campsites we have profiled in towns on Route 1 (or, for Skaftafell, just off it) in clockwise order from Reykjavík: north via Borgarnes and Akureyri, east to Egilsstaðir, down the East Fjords to Djúpivogur and Höfn, then west along the South Coast back to Reykjavík. Drive it anticlockwise by reading the table from the bottom up.[2][3]

Of the 17 profiled campsites below, 9 are open in winter, 3 are listed as winter-open with vague details (call ahead), 2 are shoulder-season only and 3 are closed in winter. The biggest gap is between Reykjavík and Akureyri, where Borgarnes (call ahead) and Blönduós (closed in winter) are so far the only campsites we have profiled: see the section on that stretch below.[4][5][6][7][8][9][10]

Scope: campsites on this site whose town is on Wikipedia's list of communities on Route 1 (plus Skaftafell, whose entrance is just off Route 1), checked 2026-11-20. Mosfellsbær is on the list, but Mosskógar campsite is in Mosfellsdalur off Route 36, so it is left out. Campsites a short detour away (for example Hellishólar, Starlight Lambafell or Farmhouse Lodge) are on the South Coast winter guide. Winter status, electricity, dump station and price columns come straight from each campsite's own page on this site.

Reykjavík to Akureyri: Borgarnes, Blönduós and the Ring Road towns not profiled yet

Between Reykjavík and Akureyri, Borgarnes – Granastaðir and Blönduós (Brautarhvammur) are the Route 1 campsites with a full page on this site so far. Other options in Ring Road towns on that stretch are below, from tjalda.is. None of them, Borgarnes and Blönduós included, is on the official camper winter service list (ferdalag.is, checked 2026-11-22).[2][17][4] (checked 2026-11-20, 2026-11-21, 2026-11-22)

  • Borgarnes – Granastaðir Campsite now has its own page (/campsites/borgarnes/) and is marked call ahead in winter: the operator's website shows 'Open now · limited service' and winter rates, and says it stays open with limited service on a best-effort basis from 15 or 20 October (the page gives both dates), while ferdalag.is and the site's own metadata give a 15 May – 15 September season and tjalda.is 10 May – 15 November. Its adult price is in conflict too: 2,000 ISK on tjalda.is and in the page text, 1,000 ISK once the operator's page has loaded in a browser.[5][16][17][18] (checked 2026-11-21)
  • Blönduós Campsite (Brautarhvammur) now has its own page (/campsites/blonduos/) and is marked closed in winter: tjalda.is gives a 2026 season of 1 January – 30 October and Parka.is 1 March – 30 October (both also show an 'Open all year' tag that contradicts those dates), and Húnabyggð's short-term lease of its town campsites ran until the end of October 2026. 2026 rates: 2,250 ISK per adult (15+), electricity 1,500 ISK, lodging tax 400 ISK per unit. The municipality plans to move the campsite to a new site at Hnjúkabyggð.[10][20][21][59][17] (checked 2026-11-21, 2026-11-22)
  • Varmahlíð (Skagafjörður): tjalda.is says this campsite is not an active participant in 2026 and that the information it shows may be outdated (it only shows 2025 rates). Its 2026 season and winter opening are Unconfirmed.[60][17] (checked 2026-11-20, 2026-11-21)

East Fjords: Breiðdalsvík

  • Between Egilsstaðir and Djúpivogur, Route 1 runs through the East Fjords towns, including Breiðdalsvík. CampEast in Breiðdalsvík shows 'Open: 29 May – 01 Sep' on tjalda.is with 2025 rates (adults 2,500 ISK, electricity 1,500 ISK per night, lodging tax 400 ISK per unit), so it is a summer-only option.[2][61][17] (checked 2026-11-20, 2026-11-21)

The Ring Road in winter

  • Mountain passes on Route 1 that can close or be difficult in winter include Hellisheiði (Reykjavík – Hveragerði), Holtavörðuheiði (Borgarnes – Staðarskáli), Vatnsskarð (Blönduós – Varmahlíð), Öxnadalsheiði (Varmahlíð – Akureyri) and Biskupsháls (Akureyri – Egilsstaðir).[2] (checked 2026-11-20)
  • Check road conditions on umferdin.is (the Road and Coastal Administration's traffic info) or by calling 1777 before every driving day, and read SafeTravel's winter driving advice.[62][63] (checked 2026-11-20)
  • From Akureyri to Djúpivogur every campsite in this guide is open in winter (Hamrar, Hlíð, Vogar, Camp Egilsstaðir, Djúpivogur). On the South Coast it is patchier: Selfoss, Skaftafell and Höfn are open all year, but Vík and Hvolsvöllur are closed in winter and Hella and Kirkjubær II are shoulder-season only.[4][31][34][38][45][56][50][46][6][7][9][8] (checked 2026-10-23, 2026-10-24, 2026-10-27, 2026-10-28, 2026-10-29, 2026-11-01, 2026-11-17)

Practical tips

  • Reykjavík Eco-Campsite: from 15 October to 15 April only Area A is open, and only for motorhomes and campervans.[13][14] (checked 2026-10-23)
  • Skaftafell is the only campsite in this guide between Kirkjubæjarklaustur and Höfn; in winter only its zones A and C stay open.[50] (checked 2026-10-23)
  • The new Route 1 section at Hornafjörður (opened 24 June 2026) also shortened the southern approach to Höfn by 14.4 km.[1] (checked 2026-11-20)
