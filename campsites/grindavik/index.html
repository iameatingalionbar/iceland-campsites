A fenced, purpose-built campsite of about 13,500 m² on Austurvegur in the fishing town of Grindavík, on the south coast of the Reykjanes Peninsula, with 42 pitches for motorhomes, caravans and trailer tents. It opened in 2009 and has a 200 m² service building (2011) with cooking facilities, showers and laundry, plus electric hook-ups, a toilet-tank disposal point and two playgrounds. After the volcanic unrest that began in late 2023 the campsite reopened; in 2026 the town of Grindavík runs it itself and reopened it on 16 March 2026. The town is open to visitors, but the Icelandic Met Office still rates Grindavík as being in considerable volcanic hazard – read the safety section below before you go.[5][4][10][1][3][8][11]

Practical tips

  • Volcanic safety: Grindavík is open to visitors, but the Icelandic Met Office (update of 10 November 2026) says land is still rising at Svartsengi and that a new magma intrusion, which could lead to an eruption on the Sundhnúkur crater row next to town, is the most likely scenario. On the hazard map in force since 30 September 2026 Grindavík is in the 'considerable' (orange) hazard category, and the warning before an eruption may be less than an hour.[8][12][11] (checked 2026-11-16)
  • If an evacuation is ordered because of a natural hazard, the town's rules say guests must follow Civil Protection's instructions and leave the campsite immediately. Check vedur.is, umferdin.is (Route 43 into town) and safetravel.is on the day.[5][8] (checked 2026-11-16)
  • No reservations – you cannot book a pitch; just turn up. Groups and family reunions pay by agreement.[1][2] (checked 2026-11-16)
  • Family-oriented rules: quiet hours 00:00–07:00, 10 km/h speed limit, no open fires or disposable grills on the ground, dogs on a leash, sort your rubbish. Minimum age for guests travelling alone is 18 (20 during the Sjóarinn síkáti festival).[5][1] (checked 2026-11-16)
  • The website www.grindavikcampsite.com given in older listings (e.g. tjalda.is) did not show campsite content when we checked on 2026-11-16 – use the town's page grindavik.is/tjaldsvaedi instead.[3][5] (checked 2026-11-16)
  • Since 2026 the town of Grindavík operates the campsite itself; the council decided this in September 2025 after an enquiry from a private operator.[10][4] (checked 2026-11-16)
